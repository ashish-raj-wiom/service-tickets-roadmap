Service Tickets: what we are building, and why

Where the service-ticket path is broken today, what we are fixing, and in what order.

Ashish Raj · PM, Service Tickets POD · 2 Sep 2026
01

Objective

When a customer's service breaks, it should be fixed quickly and without them having to chase it, stay visible to everyone working on it — and increasingly, be found before they have to tell us at all.

O1

Resolve faster, and keep everyone on the ticket able to see it

Resolve faster — and let the customer, CSP and agent all see where the ticket stands. Being unheard is a separate harm from being unresolved.

O2

Shrink the share of issues the customer has to report

Every ticket today begins with a customer contact. Move toward detecting the problem and acting before they need to call.

Scope. This starts the moment an issue exists. Preventing issues is network and infrastructure's mandate — we own what happens between an issue arising and it being genuinely resolved.

01

Information flow

O1Week 1–2

A ticket lives in four systems, and nobody working on it can see the other three. So the only tool anyone has is to phone someone — and that call is the cost of the missing link.

  • The customer cannot see any progress. The app shows no live status, so calling is the only way to find out — the exact loop we are trying to break
  • The agent cannot see the execution. Kapture holds the ticket in full, but nothing about whether a CSP accepted it, whether a technician was assigned, or when anyone will arrive. On a callback the agent can only repeat "Open"
  • What the agent learns on the call reaches nobody who can act on it. Availability and fault clues are logged as a Kapture comment that stops at Ticket Service Java — a customer home only until 11 AM gets a 12 PM visit
  • The CSP does not know the customer has already chased. Repeat-contact count is one of the strongest urgency signals we hold, and it never leaves Kapture
  • A closure made in Kapture never reaches the CSP's card. He travels to a job that no longer exists, with the clock still running

These are the gaps we know of. The pattern repeats wherever one party needs something another already holds.

02

Reopening of tickets

O1Week 3–4

A reopen is the clearest signal we get that a fix did not work — and today it is recorded, then wasted.

  • The clock restarts on a reopen. It creates a new complaint with a brand-new deadline, so the original breach never materialises
  • The CSP is never told it is a reopen. SRS stores the chain root, parent ticket and reopen count; the event it sends the CSP app ships none of them, so a repeat is worked as a fresh fault
  • Quality scores each ticket on its own, not the original problem. That makes marking resolved a consequence-free exit — one bad outcome becomes two clean-looking ones
  • It is undecided whether a reopen should count against the CSP at all. Whichever way it goes, it changes their pay

The lineage is already captured — nothing reads it.

Ticket reopen rateClosure TAT after a ticket is reopened
03

CSP said resolved vs actually resolved

O1Week 5

The connectivity check cannot fail a resolution. A negative verdict still closes the ticket as resolved and system-verified.

  • A check that cannot fail is a measurement, not a gate. Until it can block closure, "verified" in our data is not a claim we can stand behind
  • We do not know whether ping-up means the problem is gone. A line can be up at the moment we check and still be slow or dropping — and the same signal underwrites proactive detection later
  • When the check gives no verdict, there is no path to the customer. The chat confirmation loop exists but is not triggered by an inconclusive check, so a silent customer is read as success
Metrics this moves
% resolved tickets carrying a verification verdictFalse-resolution rate — ping down / check failedCustomer issue truly resolved vs CSP-reported resolved
04

Proactive detection of issues

O2Week 6–7

Every ticket today starts with a customer contact. Nothing in the system looks for a problem before someone reports it.

  • Nothing feeds the anomaly path. SRS already carries the scaffolding — classes, executors and deadlines exist — and no telemetry reaches it
  • Neither the customer nor the CSP hears anything until the customer initiates. Even where we could know first, there is no way to say so
  • We cannot yet tell a real fault from a false alarm. A proactive ticket on a healthy connection costs trust and CSP time, and we have no measure of how often that would happen
Metrics this moves
# issues the system detected before the customer reported it# service tickets reported by the customer
05

Outage handling

O2Week 8

Outages and service tickets are unlinked. At scale this is where detection either pays off or buries the CSP.

  • One outage produces many ungrouped tickets, on a path that already skips duplicate detection
  • There is no way to clear them together. TAS is entirely per-candidate, so an outage is worked one ticket at a time
  • An outage ticket carries the same deadline as an individual fault, so the CSP is judged against a clock the situation makes impossible

# outages detected# outage-ticket resolution TAT
06

Service guardrails — who owns what

O1TBD

Why this sits last. This is the fundamental one — fixing it lets future services work in sync instead of us patching around each other. It is last only because it moves the objective less directly than the items above, not because it matters less.

Chaotic today. Responsibilities overlap, and no one owns the boundaries between services.

  • Two systems compute the deadline independently and neither reads the other — a ticket raised at 21:43 promises the customer 01:43 while the CSP is measured against 15:00 the next day
  • Four systems hold a status for the same ticket, in four vocabularies, with no shared word for the same moment
  • Service tickets run an 11am–9pm working window; the rest of the estate runs 9am–9pm. Nobody has decided whether that difference is intended
  • Where two services own the same thing, no one owns the boundary. Adding a ticket type needs coordinated edits in three places, and a miss is silent
