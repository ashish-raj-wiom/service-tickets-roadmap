Service Tickets POD · Roadmap

Service Tickets: what we are building, and why

A customer's issue should be found early, fixed fast, and visible to everyone working on it. Today none of those three hold. This is the plan to change that.

Ashish Raj · PM, Service Tickets POD · 2 Sep 2026
01

Objective

When a customer's service breaks, Wiom should find out first, act before they have to ask, and — while it is being fixed — keep everyone able to see what is happening.

O1

Shrink the share of issues the customer has to report

Every ticket today begins with a customer contact. Move toward detecting the problem and acting before they need to call.

O2

Make the reported path shorter, and visible to everyone on it

Resolve faster — and let the customer, CSP and agent all see where the ticket stands. Being unheard is a separate harm from being unresolved.

Scope. This starts the moment an issue exists. Preventing issues is network and infrastructure's mandate — we own what happens between an issue arising and it being genuinely resolved.

Three ways in, all converging on one relay before anything reaches the CSP. Nothing in this path is proactive — every ticket starts with a customer contact.

03

Roadmap

01–04 are repair — a reported path that is broken today. 05–06 are new capability. Each carries the measure that tells us it worked; where that measure does not exist yet, building it is part of the work.

01

Information flow

O2

The largest gap. Once a ticket exists, none of the three parties can see what the others are doing.

  • Customer → status. The app shows no live progress, so calling is the only way to find out — the exact loop we are trying to break
  • Agent → ticket. On a callback the agent sees no status and no CSP progress, and cannot reach the partner
  • Agent → CSP. Availability, severity and fault clues learned on the call never reach the person fixing it
  • CSP → customer. The CSP has no idea this customer has already chased three times — one of the strongest urgency signals we hold
Metrics this moves
▼ CSP PN Delivery Rate · 90.8%+ Status visibility across all 3 parties+ Repeat contacts per ticket

PN delivery is the only visibility metric we have today — ~135 tickets a day where the CSP is never pinged. Nothing measures what the agent or the customer can see.

02

Reopening of tickets

O2

A reopen arrives looking like a brand-new problem, so the CSP works it from scratch.

  • Carry the lineage. A reopen should reach the CSP as a repeat attempt on the same fault, with what was already tried
  • Fix idempotency. One ticket can currently become two complaints — two deadlines, and Quality OS counts the CSP's outcome twice
  • Decide attribution first. Whether a reopen is scored against the CSP changes their pay. That call is made before anything ships, not after
Metrics this moves
+ Reopen rate by subtype+ Complaints per ticket▲ Reopen SRS–TAS Match Rate · 100%

The reopen metric we have checks that two systems agree, not how many reopens happened. Reopen rate itself does not exist — build it first; it is also the measure for item 03.

03

Verification of resolved tickets

O2

The connectivity check cannot fail a resolution — a negative verdict still closes the ticket as resolved and system-verified.

  • Let verification block closure. A check that cannot fail is a measurement, not a gate — until it can, "verified" is not a claim we can stand behind
  • Use ping data to confirm the fix. The same signal that detects a fault can confirm it is gone. This pairs directly with the item above
  • Close the customer loop. The chat confirmation already works — extend it so a denial reopens the ticket directly, and so a silent customer is not read as success
Metrics this moves
+ False-resolution rate+ Verification pass/fail split▲ Ticket Close Match · 99.3%

Nothing counts how often verification returns a negative and the ticket closes anyway — today that outcome is indistinguishable from a clean fix in our data.

04

Service guardrails — who owns what

O2

Chaotic today. Two systems compute the deadline, four hold a status, and no one owns the boundaries between them.

  • Write a responsibility charter. Where two services own the same thing, pick one and delete the other rather than reconciling them
  • One TAT calculator, every surface. A ticket raised at 21:43 promises the customer 01:43 while the CSP is measured against 15:00 the next day — same ticket, two promises
  • One public ticket ID and one status vocabulary. The customer quotes one number, the CSP works another. Services can keep internal IDs; what a person sees should be uniform
  • One working-hour window. Service tickets run 11am–9pm; the rest of the estate runs 9am–9pm. Align it, or state why this work is different
Metrics this moves
▲ TAT Calculation Accuracy · 99.8%▲ Match Rate · 99.9%▲ Enrichment Rate · 98.9%+ Customer-promised vs CSP-measured deadline gap

These reconciliation metrics score high because they check each service against itself. Success here means needing fewer of them, not raising them — nothing today compares the two deadlines a single ticket carries.

05

Proactive detection of issues

O1

Every ticket today starts with a customer contact. Nothing in the system looks for a problem before someone reports it.

  • Feed telemetry into classification. SRS already carries the anomaly scaffolding — classes, executors and deadlines exist, and nothing feeds them. This is the single unlock
  • Tell both parties early. Notify the customer and the CSP that we believe something is wrong, with an ETA, before either has to ask
  • Precision decides whether this ships. A proactive ticket on a healthy connection costs real trust and real CSP time — false-positive rate is a gate, not a footnote
Metrics this moves
+ % issues detected before customer contact+ Contacts avoided per detection+ False-positive rate — guardrail▲ Tickets Created · 1,465/day baseline

Entirely new measurement. The false-positive rate is a ship gate, not a footnote — a proactive ticket on a healthy connection costs real trust and real CSP time.

06

Outage handling

O1

Outages and service tickets are unlinked. At scale this is where detection either pays off or buries the CSP.

  • Club tickets from one outage instead of many ungrouped ones on a path that already skips duplicate detection
  • One-tap bulk resolution. TAS is entirely per-candidate today — this is what keeps detection from becoming a burden
  • A deadline that reflects an outage rather than an individual fault, so the CSP is not judged against a clock the situation makes impossible
Metrics this moves
+ Tickets per outage incident+ CSP load during an outage▼ NO_INTERNET P99 TAT · 126h vs 4h target

The P99 long tail is where repeat contacts already live — medians look healthy at 2.7h while the worst tickets sit for days. This is the honesty check on item 05.

The through-line. Items 01–04 make the reported path work as people already assume it does. Items 05–06 move us off it. The order matters: detection built on a path we cannot yet see into, measure, or trust would scale the problem rather than the fix.
