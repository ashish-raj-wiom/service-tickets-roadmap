Service Tickets: what we are building, and why

Where the service-ticket path is broken today, what we are fixing, and in what order.

Ashish Raj · PM, Service Tickets POD · 2 Sep 2026
01

Objective

When a customer's service breaks, it should be fixed quickly and stay visible to everyone working on it the whole way through — and increasingly, be found before the customer has to tell us at all.

O1

Resolve faster, and keep everyone on the ticket able to see it

Resolve faster — and let the customer, CSP and agent all see where the ticket stands. Being unheard is a separate harm from being unresolved.

O2

Shrink the share of issues the customer has to report

Every ticket today begins with a customer contact. Move toward detecting the problem and acting before they need to call.

Scope. This starts the moment an issue exists. Preventing issues is network and infrastructure's mandate — we own what happens between an issue arising and it being genuinely resolved.

03

Roadmap

01–03 are repair — a reported path that is broken today. 04–05 are new capability, and 06 is foundational. Each item names the measures it moves — and of the ten below, only two are tracked today. Building the measurement is part of the work. Timelines are indicative — a sequencing view, not committed dates.

01

Information flow

O1Week 1–2

The problem. A ticket lives in four systems, and nobody working on it can see the other three. So the only tool anyone has is to phone someone — and that call is the cost of the missing link.

What it costs today — three examples of many
1 · The customer who calls three times

Reports no internet at 9:43 PM, told 1:43 AM. Calls at 11 AM — the agent sees only "Open", and cannot say whether a CSP accepted it or when anyone will arrive. Calls again at 4 PM, same sentence. Three calls, no new information — and the CSP never learns they chased.

2 · The CSP who travels to a finished job

The line recovers on its own and the agent marks it resolved in Kapture. That closure never reaches the CSP's app. He travels to a completed job, and the clock still runs against him. ~39 tickets a day.

3 · The context we capture, then never move

The customer is home only until 11 AM. The agent logs it as a Kapture comment — which reaches Ticket Service Java and stops there. The CSP arrives at 12 PM to an empty house. We had it written down the whole time; we just never moved it.

The pattern underneath
The customer cannot see progressThe app shows no live status, so calling is the only way to find out — the exact loop we are trying to break
The agent cannot see the workKapture holds the ticket in full, but nothing about the execution — whether a CSP accepted it, whether a technician was assigned, or when anyone will arrive
The agent cannot brief the CSPAvailability, severity and fault clues learned on the call have no field to travel in
The CSP cannot see the historyNo sight of how many times this customer has already chased — one of the strongest urgency signals we hold

Every one of these compounds wherever a party needs something another already holds — the examples above are illustrations, not the scope of the work.

02

Reopening of tickets

O1Week 3–4

A reopen is the clearest signal we get that a fix did not work. Today it is recorded, and then wasted.

  • What happens today. A reopen creates a new complaint with its own fresh deadline — SRS recomputes the TAT from the reopen moment, so the clock starts again at zero
  • The CSP is never told it is a reopen. SRS does store the lineage — chain root, parent ticket, reopen count — but the event it sends the CSP app carries only the deadline. The CSP works what looks like a brand-new fault, from scratch
  • Which makes reopening a consequence-free exit. Quality scores each ticket on its own rather than the original problem. A CSP near a breach can mark it resolved; the reopen becomes a separate ticket with a clean clock, and neither ticket looks bad
  • Decide attribution first. Whether a reopen is scored against the CSP changes their pay. That call is made before anything ships, not after

The lineage is already captured — nothing reads it. This is a plumbing and scoring change, not a new data problem.

03

CSP said resolved vs actually resolved

O1Week 5

The connectivity check cannot fail a resolution — a negative verdict still closes the ticket as resolved and system-verified.

  • Let verification block closure. A check that cannot fail is a measurement, not a gate — until it can, "verified" is not a claim we can stand behind
  • Use ping data to confirm the fix. The same signal that detects a fault can confirm it is gone. This pairs directly with the item above
  • Establish that ping-up actually means fixed. A green ping is not proof the customer's problem is gone — a line can be up at the moment we check and still be slow or dropping. Measure how often a system-verified ticket comes back before trusting the check to gate closure. The same signal underwrites proactive detection later, so proving it here pays twice
  • Ask the customer only when the system cannot tell. Where the ping check gives a clear verdict, trust it; where it cannot, the customer becomes the verifier. The chat loop already works — it needs the right trigger, not a rebuild

% resolved tickets carrying a verification verdictFalse-resolution rate — ping down / check failedCustomer issue truly resolved vs CSP-reported resolved
04

Proactive detection of issues

O2Week 6–7

Every ticket today starts with a customer contact. Nothing in the system looks for a problem before someone reports it.

  • Feed telemetry into classification. SRS already carries the anomaly scaffolding — classes, executors and deadlines exist, and nothing feeds them. This is the single unlock
  • Tell both parties early. Notify the customer and the CSP that we believe something is wrong, with an ETA, before either has to ask

05

Outage handling

O2Week 8

Outages and service tickets are unlinked. At scale this is where detection either pays off or buries the CSP.

  • Club tickets from one outage instead of many ungrouped ones on a path that already skips duplicate detection
  • One-tap bulk resolution. TAS is entirely per-candidate today — this is what keeps detection from becoming a burden
  • A deadline that reflects an outage rather than an individual fault, so the CSP is not judged against a clock the situation makes impossible

06

Service guardrails — who owns what

O1TBD

Why this sits last. This is the fundamental one — fixing it lets future services work in sync instead of us patching around each other. It is last only because it moves the objective less directly than the items above, not because it matters less.

Chaotic today. Two systems compute the deadline, four hold a status, and no one owns the boundaries between them.

  • Write a responsibility charter. Where two services own the same thing, pick one and delete the other rather than reconciling them
  • One TAT calculator, every surface. A ticket raised at 21:43 promises the customer 01:43 while the CSP is measured against 15:00 the next day — same ticket, two promises
  • One working-hour window. Service tickets run 11am–9pm; the rest of the estate runs 9am–9pm. Align it, or state why this work is different
